What kind of world do you want to belong to?

Thirty hectares of coastal wallum heath on Simpson's Creek, Brunswick Heads — the last one per cent left in Byron Shire. It carries a housing approval written in 2006. We are raising $35 million to buy it instead, and hold it in perpetuity.

Not empty land. A living world.

Low, acidic, tea-coloured sand country — the kind of ground that looks like nothing until you learn to read it. Never sand-mined, never farmed. Almost nothing can live in water this acid. What does, lives nowhere else.

9 Federally listed
threatened species Koala, wallum sedge frog, Mitchell's rainforest snail, regent honeyeater, swift parrot — and four more
12 NSW-listed
threatened species Including the south-eastern glossy black-cockatoo and the long-nosed potoroo
3 Threatened
ecological communities Swamp sclerophyll forest, swamp oak floodplain forest, coastal saltmarsh

These are not ornaments on a landscape. They are the landscape. A wallum sedge frog cannot be moved to a nicer wetland, because there is no nicer wetland — it breeds only in water this acid, in sedges that grow only in sand this poor. There are old-growth scribbly gums here. Lose the ground and you lose all of it at once.

Mythology above. Institutional credibility underneath.

A story this large only works if the structure beneath it is boring, checkable and real. Here is the structure.

Registered charity CLAI is registered with the ACNC and holds deductible gift recipient status. Gifts of $2 or more are tax deductible in Australia.
Transparent governance The 2024–25 Annual Report and Financial Statement is published in full, not summarised.
Permanent stewardship The land will be held in a Conservation Land Trust, preserving its ecological values in perpetuity.
A management plan Written with qualified ecological and cultural advisers, incorporating First Nations knowledge, led with Bundjalung Traditional Owners.
